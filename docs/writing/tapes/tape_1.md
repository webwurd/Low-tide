**Where the player finds it:** Day 2. Taped inside the hatch of the warm acoustic buoy, west echo cave. The player plays it on the cottage cassette player.
**Who is speaking:** Walt Haskell, keeper, speaking to his dead wife June. January 1976, about 14 months after she died.

## LOCKED: facts the story needs
- Addressed to June. The word 'June' must appear (a test checks for it).
- Recorded at the buoy; he leaves the tape in the hatch.
- The Navy's letter says June was never on the manifest and no cable work was scheduled. That is a lie. Walt does not believe it.
- The Navy has left. The buoy is still warm, though they said it would die.
- The buoy hears a ping from the reef every 14 seconds. The Navy says it is a 'test tone'.
- Walt is not a talker. He is tender, wry and embarrassed to be talking to a machine.

### AI DRAFT (reference only)
```text
[Click. Wind in a stone cave. Somewhere deeper in, water moves against rock.]
June. It's the nineteenth of January, nineteen seventy-six. I'm in the west cave, at your buoy, because I couldn't think of anywhere else to put you.
I tried the cottage. I set the machine on the kitchen table, and every evening I sat down to tell you about my day, and every evening I found I'd been talking to the stove for an hour.
You used to say the only thing I ever said into a microphone was "Is this on?" [A short, unsteady laugh.] Well. It's on.
The Navy men left on the Tuesday boat. They took the good coffee, and the good chairs, and the cable charts. They left me a box of paperwork and a letter.
I'll read you the letter. "The Department regrets the loss of Mrs. June Haskell, civilian contractor, lost at sea in weather on the fourth of November, nineteen seventy-four. Mrs.
Haskell does not appear on the manifest for that date. No cable work was scheduled."
You never went to sea in your life, June. You went to the reef, with a red toolbag and an egg sandwich, and I watched you go.
I've read that letter four times. I read it to the stove. The stove had no opinion either.
The array's finished, they say. The cable stays in the ground, because that's cheaper than pulling it. Lieutenant Hayes signed that line himself. He has beautiful handwriting.
[Water drips. Walt breathes out.] I walked out at low water to see whether your buoy had gone dead, the way they promised. It hasn't. It's warm as a loaf, June.
I put my hand on the casing and it's warm.
And every fourteen seconds, listen, it picks up something off the reef.
[A faint electronic ping, very far off.]
[Under his breath.] ...nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen...
[A second ping, exactly where he said it would be.]
I asked Hayes what it was. He said a test tone. I said a test of what, now the array's finished? He said I wouldn't follow it. He's right about that. I don't.
I'm going to leave this in the hatch with the battery, where it's dry. I can't keep it at the cottage. If I keep it I'll play it every night, and then I'll have nothing new to say to you.
Goodnight, June. [A long breath.] I'll bring you the next one at the thaw. [Click.]
```
