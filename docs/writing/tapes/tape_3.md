### AI DRAFT (reference only)
```text
[Click. A kettle ticking as it cools. The wind is down. The room is small and close.]
June. Three years, near enough. I'm doing this one in the cottage. I've stopped taking them to the cave.
The Navy sends a man twice a year to look at your buoy now, and I'd rather he found a battery and nothing else.
A man came on the Tuesday boat with a briefcase. A commander. I didn't catch the name, which I expect was the idea. Very polite. He took his cap off in the kitchen.
He had a settlement. Four pages, and a fifth for my signature. A pension. A letter saying the Navy regrets. And one line, near the bottom, about not discussing the matter.
I asked him what I'd be not discussing. He said the schedule. I said, what schedule? He said it was classified.
So there was a schedule. [A dry breath.] I said, your schedule gave her two hours at the reef, and the table gave her one. And he didn't say a word, June. I've never been so glad of a man's silence.
I said Tom Dunmore ferried her out, and Tom says one hour. He said Tom was mistaken. He said it kindly. That's the part I can't forgive. He said it kindly.
I didn't sign. I turned the fifth page face down on the table and gave him a cup of tea, because he'd come a long way, and he drank it, and he left on the same boat.
Ruth writes. Your sister. She wrote again last week, from Providence. Every letter asks me the same thing: what did June say, the last time you saw her? And every time I put the pen down.
It was "don't wait up." That's what you said, at the landing, with your toolbag. Don't wait up. [Pause.] And I waited all night with the lamp and the radio, and nobody told me anything until noon.
I don't know how to put that in a letter to a woman who loved you nearly as much as I did.
I'll answer her. I will. Some day I'll put these in an envelope and Ruth can hear it all, the Navy and the tea and me. But not tonight. The kettle's gone cold.
Goodnight, June. [Click.]
```
