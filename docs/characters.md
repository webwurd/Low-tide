# Characters: the voice bible

Canon facts come from `narrative_outline.md` and the tapes in `dialogue/dialogue.json`. Anything marked **(proposed)** is new and needs sign-off before it appears in text. Anything not marked is already canon.

**The rule for every line:** it should be something only this person would say, in this situation, because of what they have lost. If a line could be swapped between Clara and Arthur, rewrite it.

**Global style rules**
- 1998 Maine. Plain speech. No quips that sound like a 2020s sitcom ("big bucks"), no therapy vocabulary ("process," "boundaries," "space").
- Nobody states their feelings outright when a concrete object, task or weather detail can carry them. Grief shows up as procedure.
- The radio is the only intimacy. Lines are short because the channel is shared, open and half-public. Longer speech is rare, and it means something.
- Silence is a line. "..." is allowed, but only when the next line pays it off.

## 1. Arthur Pell (proposed surname), the player character

**Who he is.** 41. A schoolteacher (history and general science) from inland Maine, a town with a mill and no coast. His wife **Maren** died in the autumn. He took the winter post because the house had become unbearable, and because it was the one job where nobody would speak to him except by radio. He is not a sailor. He is competent at paying attention.

**What he wants.** To get through the winter without being asked how he is. Underneath: to be useful to someone again, and to be told the truth by someone, because the last months were full of kind lies.

**What he fears.** Being comforted. Being "the widower." Finding out, a second time, that someone he trusted was managing him.

**The wound.** In Maren's last weeks, everyone said she was doing better. He believed them because he wanted to. He has since become an obsessive checker of facts: tide tables, barometer, small print. Honesty is not a virtue to him; it is the only thing he can still afford.

**Contradiction that makes him human.** He demands honesty from Clara, yet he withholds his own grief from her until Day 4. He notices when other people hide things and does not notice that he does it too. This is where the trust system gets its edge: the player's reports and withholds should sometimes feel like Arthur protecting himself, not like a morality test.

**How he thinks.** Like a teacher: he explains things to himself, makes lists, corrects imprecision, and asks the next question rather than the emotional one. He catalogues the island the way he'd prepare a lesson. When moved, he gets *more* precise, not less.

**How he sounds**
- Short, dry, factual sentences. Dates and numbers come naturally to him.
- Asks real questions: "What time did he say?" "Whose handwriting?" He does not say "Interesting."
- Understatement is his humour. A line like "Lamp's steady, weights are wound. The coffee is a matter of opinion" suits him. Anything louder does not.
- Never says "I feel." Says "I noticed" or "I keep thinking about."
- Avoids Maren's name until he can't. When he does use it, he uses it flatly, with a fact attached (the date, what she was reading), never an adjective.

**Tells and habits**
- Writes things down to avoid feeling them (the logbook is therapy he doesn't call therapy).
- Checks the watch when he's upset.
- Talks to the tapes. He answers Walt out loud once, then pretends he didn't.
- Winds the clock weights a little too carefully, as if someone would check.

**Reactions to the story (so he's not a blank)**
- *Tape 1 and Walt:* recognition. A man talking to his dead wife on a machine. Arthur does not remark on it; he turns the tape off earlier than he needs to, then plays it again.
- *Tape 2 (June):* professional respect. He hears a careful woman and hates the word "careless" that the Navy used. His anger is on June's behalf, never self-righteous.
- *Tape 4 (Walt's last):* "Leave it be. Maybe someone needs it more than I did." This is the line that gets him. He should say nothing back.
- *Tape 5 (Tom):* the one that makes him stand up and go outside.
- *Clara's confession:* disappointment, then relief, then a flash of anger that he has to put down. He is allowed to be angry for a moment.

**Never**
- Quips in a crisis, or jokes that deflect Clara's grief.
- Speeches about justice. He doesn't believe the world is fair. He believes records matter.
- Therapy language.

**Sample lines**
- "Tide table says one hour. I'd rather not find out about the second."
- "I teach eighth-grade history. I know what it looks like when someone's rewritten the date."
- "Maren read in the evenings. I'd read to her when she couldn't. I don't know why I'm telling you that."
- To Clara, honest option: "I didn't tell you at noon. I wanted to be sure it was real first."
- To Clara, withholding option (sympathetic): "It's the first thing I've found out here that's mine. I'd like to look at it a little longer."

## 2. Clara Dunmore (surname follows from her father Tom; not yet stated in text)

**Who she is.** About 28. Coast Guard radio dispatcher at Station Penobscot, mainland Maine. Daughter of boatswain **Tom Dunmore**. She works the night-heavy rota and is very good at a job that consists of staying calm for strangers. She has no partner and a lot of acquaintances. She is warm in a way that is mostly professional habit.

**What she wants.** For the buoy to keep pinging. For Arthur to be a good keeper who never asks the question, so she doesn't have to answer it. When he does ask, she wants to be forgiven without having to explain.

**What she fears.** That her father's hour of silence was cowardice, and that she is repeating it. That if she stops listening, June is lost twice.

**The wound.** She watched her father go quiet. After 1974 he sat in the dark with the radio on and "the sea owed him something." She became a dispatcher: the person who always answers the call. The buoy is her one secret break from procedure.

**Contradiction that makes her human.** She is the person who tells everyone else to leave the bar and get home, and she is risking her career every winter for a battery. She is honest in small things and evasive in the one big thing. She lectures Arthur about the tide and hides her own.

**How she thinks.** In procedure. Get the facts, confirm location, give the instruction, stay on the line. Under stress she gets *calmer and shorter*. Her warmth leaks out through practical care (food, warm socks, "have you eaten"), not through feeling words.

**How she sounds**
- Radio discipline: clipped, calm, confirms what she heard. "Copy that." "Say again." "Stand by." She drops it when she is genuinely moved, and the drop is the signal.
- Dry, local humour, never cute. "Watch the clock like it owes you money." "Jealous. All I've got is burnt machine coffee and static."
- Gives orders as favours: "Do me a kindness and stay off the bar."
- Steady use of *Arthur* by name when she is worried, never when she is joking.
- Does not apologise on the radio. She says "That's on me" or goes silent.
- Local detail. She has opinions about the Penobscot ferry, the tide at the Narrows, and what happens to coffee at 3 a.m.

**Tells and habits**
- Asks "Have you eaten?" when she means "Are you all right?"
- Changes the subject to weather exactly when a question gets close.
- Keeps her father's pocket tide table on the console. She never mentions it until Day 6.
- Hums nothing, taps a pencil, stops when she realises the channel is open.

**Her voice across the trust tiers (the Day 6 test)**
| Tier | How she sounds | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Low (stonewalled)** | Clipped, formal, full radio-voice. "Penobscot out." | She retreats into procedure. Never cruel. Cold as a locked door. |
| **Mid (partial)** | Guarded but human; sentences begin and stop. | Gives facts, withholds feeling. "My father was on the boats." Leaves the rest. |
| **High (admitted)** | Slows down, uses plain words, stops the radio habits. | She says *less* than the current script does, and lets silence do the work. |

**Her reaction to being caught (important)**
- First: anger, quick and quiet. "That wasn't yours to find."
- Then: relief she doesn't want to show.
- She never begs. She asks Arthur, once, what he's going to do, and accepts the answer.

**Never**
- Mother-hen cliché, baby talk, "sweetie."
- Exposition dumps ("You've earned this. My father piloted the boat..."). She says the minimum and Arthur supplies the rest from the tapes.
- Flirting. The bond is two lonely people on a channel, not a romance. Tenderness yes; courtship no.
- Filler agreement ("Alright, Arthur." "Then do it."). If she has nothing to add, she says less.

**Sample lines**
- "Say again? ... Copy. Stay put, Arthur. I mean it."
- "I'm not asking if you're all right. I'm asking if you've eaten."
- "My father kept the log. I kept the battery. We're not a family that says things."
- High-trust confession, trimmed: "He said one hour. They said two. He was there. That's all I'm going to say about that night. The buoy's mine. I told myself it was her recorder."
- "If I stop listening, nobody is." (echoing tape 6, so use sparingly)

## 3. Walt Haskell, keeper, 1974 to 1979 (voice on tapes 1, 3, 4)

**Who he is.** Born about 1914, a lifelong keeper, hosts the Pelagic project and trusts the Navy until it costs him June. A gentle, practical, bookish man who is not articulate and knows it. He talks to a tape recorder because he can't talk to a person.

**Voice.** Tender and a little rambling. Domestic details first (kettle, coffee, the Tuesday boat), the wound second, sideways. He jokes at his own expense. He uses her name as punctuation: "June." He never states the loss; he lists what is left (the coffee, the paperwork).

**What he wants.** For June to be written down accurately. He refuses the settlement because "what would I be not discussing?" is a keeper's question.

**Tells.** "[A long breath.]" is his comma. He talks to her as if she's in the next room. His ending ritual is "Goodnight, June."

**Never.** Rage speeches. He is quietly stubborn, not furious.

**Sample line.** "I keep telling myself you'd laugh at me, talking to a machine."

## 4. June Haskell, acoustician (voice on tape 2)

**Who she is.** Mid-30s, a scientist and the cleverest person on the rock. Precise, impatient with bureaucracy, fond of Walt. She is on tape for eleven minutes and the player must love her anyway.

**Voice.** Professional first, then human in side-remarks. She names the date and splice number, then lets slip "Walt, if you're listening over your coffee, I know, I know." Quick, dry, a little teasing. She calls the situation as she sees it: "That's... earlier than the table said. Well." Never panics on tape; the tension is in what the audience knows.

**Detail to preserve.** "The left pocket. He'll know." Never explain it (nothing in Arthur's present should decode it until the player finds the item). Optional payoff: the recorder's left-pocket token (a small, intimate object Walt gave her) is something Ruth could recognise.

**Never.** Foreshadowing speeches, melodrama, or heroics.

## 5. Tom Dunmore, boatswain (voice on tape 5; present through Clara)

**Who he is.** Coast Guard boatswain on the Pelagic boats; Clara's father; dead by 1997. He told the Navy one hour. He held the boat off the reef with the engine running and watched the water come over the bar. After that he stopped going out.

**Voice.** Military log format. "Oh-six-fifty." "Holding station off the reef." He uses procedure as armour, and the break is one flat sentence: "I said one hour." No adjectives. No "I'm sorry."

**What he is to Clara.** A man who sat in the dark with the radio on. Clara quotes him rarely and exactly. Whatever she says about him should be something he actually did, not a summary of his feelings.

**Never.** Tearful monologues. Tom breaks by going shorter.

## 6. Maren (Arthur's wife; never speaks, only remembered)

**Who she was (proposed).** A librarian or a reader in any case: someone who read in the evenings. She had a particular chair. Her last autumn was long. Specifics should be small and domestic (a bookmark, her hand on the radiator, a Thursday ritual). She is never a saint and never a plot device.

**Rule.** Maren appears only through Arthur's small objects and facts. No flashback scenes, no voice on tape. She echoes through what the tapes make him think about: Walt's "I don't know what to say to a woman who loved you as much as I did" should land on Arthur without comment.

**Open decision.** Cause of death. Leave unstated unless you want a decision; "autumn" and "I couldn't stay in the house" are enough. If stated, make it ordinary (illness), not tragic spectacle.

## 7. Supporting names (no on-screen voice)

- **Ruth Whitcomb**, June's sister, Wickenden Street, Providence. Known only through Walt's tapes ("the only one who ever asked") and the epilogue. If she ever speaks, she is plain, wary, and unsentimental. The endings should treat her with respect and not as a prize.
- **Lt. Hayes**, USN. Never heard. A schedule and a signature. Keep him faceless; the harm is in the paperwork, not in a villain.

## How the two leads play against each other

| | Arthur | Clara |
|---|---|---|
| Armour | Facts, lists, the logbook | Procedure, the radio voice |
| Comfort | Precision | Practical care (food, warmth) |
| Lie of choice | "Nothing to report." | "It's the job." |
| Big secret | His grief, held back until Day 4 | The buoy, and her father, held back until Day 6 |
| What breaks them open | A tape that sounds like his own life | Being found out by someone who doesn't punish her |
| What they never say | "I need help" | "I'm sorry" |

**Dynamic.** Neither of them asks the real question out loud until late. Intimacy grows through small practical exchanges: the weather, the stove, the lamp. The player should feel the trust shift in the *quality of the silences and the shortening of sentences*, not just in a hidden integer.

**The Day 4 trade** (Clara's father for Arthur's wife) is the emotional centre of the game. Both reveals should be short, concrete and slightly clumsy. No reply should tidy it up.

## Revision checklist for any new line
1. Could another character say this? If yes, rewrite.
2. Does it name an emotion? Replace with an object, task or number.
3. Is it longer than it needs to be on a shared radio channel?
4. Does it explain what the player already knows from a tape? Cut it.
5. For Clara: does the radio habit drop only when she is genuinely moved?
6. For Arthur: is he precise, dry, curious, and unwilling to be comforted?
